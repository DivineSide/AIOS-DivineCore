Writing guide

Re-read before drafting. Collapsed by design — open what you need.

Condensed from linkedin-playbook.md §13 — the source of truth. Update there too.

⚡ Pre-post check — if a draft is weak, it's one of these
  1. The insight isn't interesting → rework the insight
  2. The idea isn't original (copied) → find your own angle
  3. The hook is weak → rewrite the opening
  4. The body is hard to read → simpler structure, shorter sentences
Core principle — story, not features §13.1
Don't list features. Make the reader envision the experience they'll have if they apply what you're teaching, and the feeling that comes with it.

The most powerful posts follow an ordinary-to-extraordinary arc: a normal person levels up and transforms against the odds. Before-and-after.

If your audience has a problem, package the insight as a story about the problem, not a checklist about the solution.
The 6-step construction process §13.2
1 · Come up with the INSIGHT (not the topic)
Don't copy others' insights — you become a copycat and lose the texture only you can write. Mine your own: talk to clients/prospects (their problems, questions, dreams); take notes on what you do daily, especially building in public; analyse your back story.
2 · Clarify the idea
One post = one idea. Never two — if you have two, write two posts. Package the insight as a story, not a feature list.
3 · Choose the structure
People skim, they don't read. Short, clear sentences. Pick the framework: PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution, educational top-of-funnel) · SLA (Story-Lesson-Application, personal transformation) · Before-After-Bridge. Body in 3-4 visible sections: Set the scene → Problem → Journey → Resolution. Reading level 5th-7th grade — if you wouldn't say it to a friend, rewrite it.
4 · Write the hook
Strongest formula: start with something the audience wants, then "how I [achieved it]" — but only if you actually achieved it, or credibility collapses. A good hook triggers interest, says what the post is about, makes them care, and hands them something usable. Levers: specific numbers · talk to "you" · state + agitate a problem · a surprising fact.
5 · Write the body + CTA
Deliver what the hook promised. Simple sentences. Write for ICP fit, not raw reach. End with a specific question tied to the post (never "Thoughts?"). When someone in your ICP engages, DM them: acknowledge their work, no pitch.
6 · Pick the image
In priority: 1) before-and-after photos · 2) an authentic photo of you capturing the win (bright, vibrant) · 3) infographic/graph/screenshot with a title overlay that doubles as the hook. Carousels work for step-by-step / framework posts.
Content multiplication — double down on what works §13.3
When a topic goes viral, the audience told you what they want. Don't move on — double down. For one validated topic: write 10 posts on it, each a different story; then 10 more variations per format (text, video, photo, carousel). That's 50 posts from one validated topic.
