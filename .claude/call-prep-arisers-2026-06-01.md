# Call Prep — Arisers DMA (Joey Springer) · 2026-06-01

**Who:** Joey Springer, founder of Arisers Digital Marketing Agency. Small, founder-led ("Joey and his team"). Runs paid ads + ad creative for blue collar / home service businesses. Positioning: "Scaling blue collar businesses $40K+ in MRR through paid ads and AI-driven solutions." Result claims are all ad-side (doubled revenue, cut CPA 50%, 11 leads in 10 days).

**Goal of this call:** not to sell him. Validate a **partnership**: he runs the ads, I run the backend that catches the leads his ads generate. Test whether speed-to-lead + the other systems (reviews, database reactivation, referral, retargeting) land for blue collar, and whether he wants to move forward.

**The one sentence:** *"Your ads create the hot lead. Right now nothing catches it. I'm the half of the funnel you don't sell yet."*

**Status:** he booked then went quiet on my DMs. So I have two paths: (A) he shows to the meeting, (B) he doesn't and I cold-call / voicemail / text.

---

## The stat — say it right

Do **not** say "400%." Not cleanly sourced, and Joey runs ads for a living so he'll smell a soft number.

Use this instead (MIT / Kellogg Lead Response Management study, widely cited):
- **Respond to an inbound lead within 5 minutes and you're ~21x more likely to qualify it than if you wait 30 minutes.**
- **~78% of customers buy from the business that responds first.**

For missed calls, don't quote a hard % unless you've verified it. Say it plainly instead: *"For home service, a missed call during a job IS the lost sale. They call the next guy on Google."* That's true and undeniable, no stat needed.

# PART A — COLD CALL / VOICEMAIL (he didn't confirm)

Keep it under 60 seconds. The goal of the cold call is **not to pitch.** It's to get the meeting that's already booked to actually happen, or rebook it.

### If he picks up

> "Hey Joey, it's Pang. We've got a call booked but I figured I'd just call you direct since I know inboxes get buried. Bad time?"

*(If bad time:)* "No worries. Two minutes worth on a call later this week? I've got one specific thing about your ad clients I think you'll want to hear. When's better, today or tomorrow?"

*(If he's got a minute:)* "Quick version. You run ads for home service businesses and they work, you've got the case studies. Here's the thing I keep seeing: the ad does its job, someone calls the business for a quote, and nobody picks up. That lead's gone, they called the next guy. From your client's seat it looks like your ads underdelivered, when really it was a catching problem, not an ads problem. I build the system that catches those. I think it makes your ads look 30% better for free. Worth a proper 20 minutes this week?"

> Lock the time before you hang up. "Cool, I'll keep our [day/time] then. I'll send a calendar invite right now so it's locked."

# PART B — THE PARTNERSHIP CALL (he showed up)

Same spine as a discovery call: ask more than you pitch. But the twist: **his customer is the blue collar business. My value to Joey is making his ads convert and his clients stay.** So diagnose HIS problem (retention, results pressure, churn), not a client's.

Talk less than 40% of the time. If you're talking more, stop and ask a question.

---

## 1 · Open (2 min)

Light, human, fast. Then:

> "Before we dive in, I run an AI notetaker on these so neither of us has to scribble. I'll send you the notes after. Cool?"

> "Here's how I figured we'd use the time. First I want to actually understand your agency and your clients, because I had an idea but I want to pressure-test it against what you're seeing day to day. Then if there's something here, I'll show you exactly what I'm thinking and we figure out if it's worth doing together. Sound good?"

---

## 2 · Diagnose HIS world (8-10 min)

You're learning where his pain is. Listen for: churn, clients blaming ad performance, his own bandwidth.

- "How long you been running Arisers? Mostly home service, or a spread?"
- "When a new client comes on and the ads start working, what happens to the leads after that? Do you have eyes on what the business does with them, or does it leave your hands at the lead?"
- "What's the biggest reason a client churns on you? Is it 'ads didn't work,' or something downstream?"  *(This is the money question. Most agency churn is 'I'm not seeing ROI' even when the ads delivered leads, because the client mishandled them.)*
- "When a client says 'I'm not getting enough out of this,' how often is it actually the ads vs. them not catching what you sent?"
- "Do you do anything on the backend right now, follow-up, booking, reviews, or is it purely ads and creative?"

Then go first with numbers so he opens up:
> "I'll be straight on my side too. I build AI systems for businesses, the backend stuff, lead capture, follow-up, reactivation. I've built for an e-com brand doing £2M+ and a few SaaS companies. I'm now looking at home service because the gap there is bigger than anywhere I've seen."

---

## 3 · The leak (lead him to it, don't lecture)

Once he's told you clients churn on "results," name the gap. Make him say it.

> "Can I tell you what I think is actually happening, and you tell me if I'm wrong? Your ads are doing the job. The lead sees the ad and calls the business, or fills the form, or DMs them. That's a hot lead, someone with a broken AC in July wants it fixed today. But the owner's on a job, the phone rings out, nobody texts back, the form sits till evening. So they call the next company on Google. That lead's gone. And here's the part that hurts you: your client doesn't see 'we missed a call.' They see 'the ads aren't converting.' So they blame you and they churn. Does that match what you see?"

Let him react. Then the stat, lightly:
> "The data backs it up. Respond to an inbound lead in 5 minutes and you're about 21 times more likely to actually qualify it than if you wait half an hour. ~78% of people buy from whoever responds first. For home service it's even more brutal, because the job is urgent. A missed call isn't a follow-up-later, it's a dead sale."

---

## 4 · The wedge (the ONE thing, not the whole menu)

Lead with the single highest-leverage system. Don't dump the list yet.

> "So the first thing I'd build is dead simple and it's the one that moves the needle most: **speed-to-lead.** The second a lead comes in, from any channel, ad call, form, DM, the system texts them back within seconds. 'Hey, this is [business], saw you reached out, what do you need help with?' If a call gets missed, it instantly fires a text so the lead never hits silence. It books the appointment, qualifies them, and pings the owner. The business catches leads they were flat-out losing, from the exact same ad spend you're already running."

Then tie it back to HIS problem, not the client's:
> "What that does for you: your clients book more jobs from the same budget, so your ROI story gets stronger without you touching the ads. Your case studies get better. And the churn reason, 'I'm not seeing results,' mostly goes away, because now they're catching what you send. I'm not competing with you. I'm making the half you don't sell yet."

---

## 5 · The roadmap (only after the wedge lands)

If he's nodding, widen it. Frame as "here's everything that's possible, we'd start with one."

> "Speed-to-lead is where I'd start. But once that's in, there's a stack of systems that all do the same job, squeeze more revenue out of leads you already paid for:
> - **Reviews on autopilot** — after a job's done, auto-ask for a Google review. More reviews = higher up the map = more calls, free. Every home service business is terrible at this.
> - **Database reactivation** — they've got a list of past customers sitting dead. One campaign, 'hey, time for your annual service,' and you pull jobs out of a list that cost nothing. Pure profit, no ad spend.
> - **Referral system** — turn happy customers into a steady trickle of new ones automatically.
> - **Retargeting glue** — make sure the people your ads touched but didn't convert get followed up instead of forgotten.
>
> All of it makes your ads look better, because the same spend produces more booked jobs. I wouldn't build all of it day one. We'd prove speed-to-lead on one client first, then add."

---

## 6 · The partnership structure (keep it loose on call 1)

Don't over-engineer this live. Float the shape, let him react, decide later.

> "On how we'd work together, I'd rather not box it in today. A few ways it can go:
> - **You white-label it** — I build and run it under Arisers, you sell it as part of your offer, you mark it up. Your clients never see me.
> - **You intro, I deliver, we split** — you connect me to clients, I close and run it, you take a cut of the monthly.
> - **Bolt-on** — I sell it alongside your ads as the catching layer, you get better retention and case studies out of it.
>
> Honestly, for the first one I'd just want to prove it on a single client of yours. Pick the one where you KNOW leads are leaking. We catch them, you watch the booked jobs go up, then we decide what the real deal looks like. Low risk for you. I do the work, you see if it's real."

**Don't quote firm pricing on this call.** If he pushes: *"It's a monthly per business, lands lower than what one missed job a week costs them. I'll put exact numbers in writing once I've seen one of your clients' setup, I don't want to guess."*

---

## 7 · Objections

- **"My clients are too small / cheap for this."** → "That's exactly why it works. They can't afford to lose a single lead, and they've got no ops person to catch them. The system is the ops person. Cheaper than a part-time receptionist and it never sleeps."
- **"I just do ads, this isn't my thing."** → "Right, and that's the gap. You hand off at the lead and lose control of what happens next, but you still get blamed for it. This gives you eyes on the whole funnel and a reason clients stay. You don't build anything, I do."
- **"Can't they just use [GHL / a chatbot]?"** → "They could buy the tool. They won't set it up, tune it, or maintain it, same reason they don't answer their own phones. The tool isn't the value, the system running it is. That's what I do."
- **"Does AI texting feel spammy / will it annoy their customers?"** → "It's the opposite. Someone with a burst pipe at 9pm WANTS an instant reply. The annoyance is silence. We keep it human and it hands to a person the second it needs to."
- **"I've got no bandwidth to add something new."** → "That's the point, you don't lift anything. I build it, I run it, you just get the better numbers and the happier clients. The only thing I need from you is one client to prove it on."

---

## 8 · Close — no hard close, lock the next step

> "Here's what I'd do. Give me one client where you know leads are leaking. Let me look at how they handle inbound today, and I'll come back to you with exactly what I'd build, what it'd catch, and what the deal looks like, in writing, in a couple days. No commitment from you today. Then we reconnect [pick a specific day] and decide. Fair?"

**Book the follow-up before you hang up. Specific time. Send the invite live.**

End open:
> "Anything else on your mind before we wrap? Anything about your clients or the ads side I should know?"

That last question usually surfaces the real blocker. Whatever he says, it goes in the written follow-up.
