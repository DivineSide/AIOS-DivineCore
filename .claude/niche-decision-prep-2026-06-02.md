# Niche Decision — Cofounder Meeting (2026-06-02)

**What this meeting is for:** decide whether we move off UK ecom beauty DTC, and if so, to which niche. We are NOT locking the offer or the price tonight. We take a day or two, decide the direction tomorrow, then run a sprint.

## 1. Why I want to switch (the core argument)

Three weeks in UK ecom beauty. This is what actually happened, not a hunch.

- We ran **6 discovery calls.** Getting them was slow and hard.
- The pain that surfaced on almost every call is the **same thing: ads and content.** What to make, which content actually works, the hours it eats.
- **That is the one thing we cannot build.** The ads and content space is far more competitive, the results are volatile, and we have zero experience running ads ourselves. We would be guessing.
- Even setting ads aside, we burn most of our time just **figuring out WHAT to build** for each brand. There is no clear, repeatable offer. "A custom solution that saves you 10 hours a week" sounds weak, because we do not even know what we are building yet.
- Their **margins are thin,** so the budget for us is thin. And most are already **juggling 14 to 15 tools,** so "buy one more tool" is an uphill sell.
- **The time cost is the killer.** Building the beauty system from scratch, the thing we actually sell, realistically takes us 3 to 6 months, maybe up to a year. First we have to discover what the system even is, then build it, then prove it works. In the new niche **the playbook already exists.** Database reactivation, reviews, referrals, and speed-to-lead are known, proven workflows. We skip the entire discovery and R&D phase and go straight to selling and building. That alone saves us the better part of a year.

**The conclusion:** instead of spending more weeks hunting for an offer that might work, move to a niche where the workflow is already proven and the cash comes faster. We stop guessing and start selling.

### On the agency route specifically

Why it is worth testing, even though it is not the destination:

- **Fastest demand validation we have.** Their booking links mean we can get on ~5 calls a day with no cold outreach. We can find out whether anyone wants this in days, not weeks.
- **The stickiness argument works on every agency.** An agency's ads generate leads for its clients. Leads are hard to win. If the client then fails to reply fast, the sale dies, and the client blames the ads (not their own slow response) and churns. A system that catches those leads makes the agency's clients stickier and its results look better. That retention angle holds no matter how the agency is billed.

**The billing nuance, because it decides who actually buys:** how an agency gets paid decides how much it cares about lead response.

| Agency billing model | How much they care about lead response |
|---|---|
| **Performance / commission** (paid per sale or % of client revenue) | **Most.** Their pay depends directly on leads converting, so our system grows THEIR revenue. The sharpest target. |
| **% of ad spend, or flat monthly retainer** | Indirectly. Paid regardless of conversion, so they care only via client retention. |
| **Per-lead / cost-per-lead** | Least. Paid once the lead is delivered, so conversion is the client's problem. Care only via retention. |

**Honest caveat:** the tools here are heavily commoditized, so actually closing agencies will be hard. But validating demand is cheap and fast because the calls are so easy to book. Use the agency route to learn quickly, not as the model we bet on.

## 4. The workflows, one by one

Each JP-stack workflow has real value AND a crowded field of dedicated vendors. **Sold alone, every one is a commodity. The differentiation is the STACK, run for them, in one niche.** That is exactly what JP did.

| Workflow | Value | Competition | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Review generation** | High. 100+ reviews / 4.5 stars dominate the map pack, SMS asks open at 98%, automation doubles monthly reviews in 60 days | Brutal: Birdeye ($299-449), Podium ($399-599), NiceJob, TrueReview, Growth99, plus GHL bundles it | Real value, fully commoditized. Not the headline. |
| **Referral system** | High. Referred clients ~37% higher LTV, 92% trust word-of-mouth | Crowded: ReferralHero, Cherry, ReferralRock, spa-software built-ins | A retainer layer, not the wedge. |
| **Database reactivation** | **Highest immediate ROI.** 3-8% on dead leads, one cited campaign made $300k off under $500 spend, SMS opens 97% in 5 min | Vendors exist, but it is run as a done-for-you campaign, not a turnkey toggle, so far less commoditized | **The wedge we lead with.** |

**Why we lead with database reactivation:** it is the fastest cash, which is the whole reason we are switching. It mines money from a list the client already owns, with no new ad spend and no change to how they operate. "Give me your dead-lead list, I will book you jobs in two weeks" is a demo that closes itself. We land that, then stack reviews, referrals, and speed-to-lead on retainer, adding a layer each time the last one earns it. That is the bucket-with-holes model, pointed at a niche where we can actually build.

**Bonus:** med spas in 2026 are shifting hard to retention-driven growth (acquisition cost has doubled since 2023, 73% of revenue is repeat patients, a 5% retention lift means 25-95% more profit). The JP stack is exactly what that market is rewarding right now.

## 5. My recommendation

1. **Switch, and pick ONE direct vertical.** Med spas have the far better buyer; home services have volume but the worst margins and the most saturation. I lean med spas.
2. **Lead the offer with database reactivation** (fastest cash), then stack the rest on retainer.
3. **Win on delivery and narrowness, never on the feature.** We will never win on "we have speed-to-lead." We win on "we set it up, tune it, and run it so it actually books appointments for [one specific kind of business]." That is the only thing that is not commoditized.
4. **So we do not end up here again, we pre-commit kill criteria.** This is the answer to "what stops you switching a third time": we run the new niche for a set number of weeks and paid pilots, and we only revisit the niche if a specific failure signal hits. We agree those numbers when we lock the niche. The difference between this switch and a flip-flop is that this one is backed by 6 calls and a written rule, not a mood.

## 7. The sprint (example, for after we decide)

A 1-week, all-hands, high-volume outreach push. The point is to force the system into existence and generate real demand data fast.

- **Everyone picks one outreach motion** they will actually run: cold calls, LinkedIn outreach, or email. Whatever fits the person.
- **High volume, every day.** We are talking dozens, up to ~100 touches a day each, not 5.
- **Everyone posts their number in a shared channel daily.** A missed number gets said out loud the next morning, not hidden.
- **Sunday review:** total touches, replies, and meetings booked. Conversion math. What we keep.

One honest week of this tells us whether we execute when the system is explicit. That answer matters as much as the niche itself.
