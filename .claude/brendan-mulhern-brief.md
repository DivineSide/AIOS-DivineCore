## Who we are, in one paragraph

DivineSide builds AI systems for businesses, run by three co-founders. Most agencies build one automation and walk away. We build a connected system that runs several of a business's most painful manual functions and gets smarter on that business's own data over time. The outcome the client buys is simple: hours back, and more revenue without more ad spend or manual work. I (Pang) run sales and marketing; one co-founder owns product, the other is an all-rounder.

## The bigger vision (the why behind the builds)

The long-term product is an **AI Operating System for education businesses**: a closed-loop system that automates the most painful parts of a coaching business at once, not just one workflow. It observes what is happening in the business, compares it to what should be happening, and self-corrects, the way a thermostat does instead of a heater. We do not design this up front or sell it. We build real systems for real clients first, then harvest the patterns that repeat into the product. Delivery earns the platform, not the other way around. The endgame is to turn this into a SaaS that any education business can use, and we are already building toward it while we deliver the custom systems.

## The niche and why we chose it

**Niche: education and coaching businesses (test prep and tutoring centers).** We chose it for two reasons:

1. **A warm beachhead.** One founder has a direct connection to an established coaching-institute owner in India (8 years in business). He is more motivated to share us than we are to sell, and he has **50+ educational-business connections** he can introduce us to. India is not the highest pricing-power market, but that warm distribution channel is the fastest path to our first handful of clients.
2. **Structural fit.** A coaching business is an information business, every doubt asked, paper attempted, and result recorded is reusable, causal data. That is exactly what a learning system compounds on, which makes the niche a strong fit for what we are building underneath.

India is the validation ground. The US is the target market (5-10x the pricing power for the same product), and the US is the track I run.

## What we build (research so far)

We are still in the research phase: contacting businesses, learning their pain points, then automating the most painful parts. **We have one client in the niche so far** (the India beachhead), for whom we have already built an **automatic content generation system** (lecture notes, question papers, answer papers from a lesson plan, in their format).

The workflows we offer / are researching across the niche:

1. **Content generation** — lecture notes, question papers, answer papers, study material, generated in the institute's template. (Built for client #1; the validated #1 pain on the India side.)
2. **Reviews system** — automated Google review requests at the right moment, so the center ranks higher when parents search and gets more inbound calls.
3. **Referral system** — automated referral asks to happy parents, turning word-of-mouth into a managed channel instead of luck.
4. **Lead follow-up** — every inquiry (web form, call, SMS) gets an instant response that qualifies and books the consultation, without the owner being available.
5. **Student-finding outreach** — cold outreach on WhatsApp / call / DM to bring the institute new students.
6. Plus additional systems in our internal library (Google review auto-replies, parent communication, fee follow-up) that we layer in as we go deeper with a client.

The principle: enter through the single most painful manual function for that owner, prove value, then expand to a second and third workflow on a retainer.

## How I do LinkedIn outreach

Manual only, no automation (account-ban risk is not worth it). For ICP owners who are active on LinkedIn I run an engage-first sequence:

1. Like and leave a genuine 1-3 sentence comment on a recent post of theirs.
2. Wait 2 days, then send a blank connection request (no note, a note reads as a setup for a pitch).
3. On accept, open with a real question instead of a pitch: *"I used to study at a tuition center and basically all their new students came from word of mouth. Is it the same for you?"*
4. If it warms up, a soft, face-saving ask: *"Do you know any tutoring or test-prep owners who'd want more Google reviews and referrals automatically?"*
5. On silence, a 30-second Loom recorded while I'm on their profile (so my face is the thumbnail), no pitch, just a hello.

Daily volume: ~20 connection requests to ICP only, plus 5-8 DMs (a mix of research-question DMs and free-work DMs). Free work is a 1-page ops audit personalized to their institute plus a Loom, delivered as a single Google Doc link.

One thing I've learned: most test-prep owners are barely on LinkedIn, so I tier them (A = posts regularly, B = comments only, C = barely logs in) and route the inactive ones to email, phone, or Instagram instead.

## Cold email + Loom (built from your advice)

> Context: I also run an automated Instantly campaign for volume (541 sent so far, 5.36% reply rate, 2 positive). The manual, Loom-led motion below is the additional approach I'm building from your advice, for my best-fit and unicorn targets. The two run side by side.

> **Disclaimer:** this is my first pass at turning your advice into something concrete, so I could easily have misread or overcomplicated parts of it. Please don't hold back, I'd genuinely value any corrections or thoughts.

**The approach I landed on:**
- Manual and personal, 10-20 researched companies a day, multi-thread top-down (owner first, then head of marketing, then admissions). I'll keep my automated campaign running for volume and use this manual motion on my best-fit and "unicorn" targets, like you suggested.
- The email itself stays short. Its only job is to get them to watch a personal Loom. The Loom is where the value lives.
- In the Loom I do a mini-audit: I show one or two specific things that are costing them students (their review gap versus a nearby competitor, or slow follow-up on new inquiries), explain why it matters, but I hold back how I'd fix it and save that for the call. (This is the part I asked about in question 7.)

**The email (built around the Loom):**

> **subject:** quick question [firstName]
>
> Hey [firstName],
>
> Have you ever thought about how students who need test prep actually find and choose a center? I was that student once, and it was harder than it should be, so I built a solution to it.
>
> I recorded a quick 2-minute video for you about [Company]:
>
> [Loom link]
>
> If it's worth a deeper look, I've got Tuesday or Thursday at 2 open.
>
> Pang

**The Loom script (60-90 seconds, their site and Google listing on screen):**

> "Hey [firstName], Pang here, this'll be quick. I came across [Company] and I work with test prep and tutoring centers, so I spent a few minutes looking at how you show up online. Let me show you what I found.
>
> First thing. You've got [#] Google reviews. A center a few minutes from you, [competitor], has [#]. When a parent searches for test prep in your area, that gap is a big part of who shows up first and who gets the call.
>
> Second. I filled in your contact form yesterday and [haven't heard back / it took two days]. Every one of those is a parent who was ready to enroll and didn't get a reply in time.
>
> Now, I'm not going to get into exactly how I'd fix these on a video, because the right fix depends on your business and it's worth doing properly, together. But both of these are very fixable, and they're costing you students right now.
>
> If you want, I'll walk you through exactly what I'd do on a quick 15 minutes. Tuesday or Thursday at 2 work? Thanks for watching."

## Cold call

I'm adopting the method you laid out: get past the gatekeeper by leading with the prospect's name, reference the email I sent, pitch that I help drive new students and book a 15-minute discovery call (no selling on the phone), then the two-call close (discovery, then present a custom plan and close).

The one place I adapted it: when they ask "how does it work," I give a short clue (my one-liner, the positioning, a couple of example workflows) rather than withholding the how completely, because I'm worried that giving them zero idea leaves no reason to take the call.
