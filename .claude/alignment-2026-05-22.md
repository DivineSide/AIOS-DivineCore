## 2. How DTC ecom works + the niche we target (12 min)

Before the discovery calls, all three of us need the same operator-level picture of how an ecom DTC business actually runs. This section is that picture, plus the specific niche we draw out of it. The deeper learning happens in the homework (Section 4).

### How DTC ecom actually works (shared mental model)

**DTC = Direct-to-Consumer.** The defining feature is **customer ownership**, not the product itself. The brand sells direct on its own Shopify store and keeps the email list, SMS list, purchase history, and full marketing data. The point is not who makes what, the point is who owns the relationship.

**The end-to-end flow:**

1. **Acquisition.** Brand spends on Meta + Google + TikTok ads, influencer/UGC, organic content. Traffic lands on their Shopify store.
2. **Conversion.** Automated Shopify checkout. No human in the loop. 24/7. Customer self-serves from cart to payment. (This is the crucial difference from JP's gyms: no human stands between traffic and revenue.)
3. **Fulfillment.** Shopify routes the order to a **3PL** (third-party logistics: Huboo, James and James, ShipBob UK). The 3PL holds the brand's inventory in its warehouse, picks, packs, ships. Tracking syncs back to Shopify.
4. **Post-purchase.** Customer gets shipping emails. Order arrives. They either become a repeat customer (the entire game) or they lapse.
5. **Retention.** Email and SMS lifecycle (Klaviyo + Postscript), reviews, referrals, replenishment. **This is where the £ lives.**
6. **Customer service.** Inbound tickets via Gorgias or Zendesk. WISMO ("where's my order"), returns, complaints.

**Manufacturing happens upstream and offline.** Beauty/health DTC mostly uses UK/EU contract manufacturing (regulatory, "Made in UK" angle, not China). Manufacturer ships pallets to the 3PL on a 60-180 day lead time. That lead time is why inventory pain is brutal: the brand commits to product 3 months before it sells.

**Important correction on warehouses:** at £1-5M ARR, the brand does NOT own the warehouse. The 3PL does. The brand owns the inventory; the 3PL holds and ships it. Self-owned warehouses don't make economic sense until ~£10M ARR.

**Key metrics operators live by** (memorize, you will hear these on every call):

- **ROAS:** return on a specific ad's spend. Per-platform. Unreliable post-iOS 14.5.
- **MER:** Marketing Efficiency Ratio. Total revenue / total marketing spend. The honest version. Can't be gamed.
- **AOV:** average order value.
- **CAC:** customer acquisition cost.
- **LTV:** lifetime value.
- **Repeat purchase rate** (60-day, 90-day, 12-month). The single most important retention number.

**Benchmarks:** MER > 4 = profitable. 2.5-4 = treading water. < 2.5 = burning cash. Healthy CAC : LTV for beauty = 1 : 3 minimum.

Every operator we talk to will quote these. If we can't speak the language fluently, credibility evaporates in five seconds.

### The subtypes of ecom (and why the difference matters)

Why we draw the line where we do: **it comes down to who owns the customer relationship.** That single fact decides whether our system can even be built for them.

| Subtype | Who owns the customer | Target? |
|---|---|---|
| **Own-website DTC (Shopify-primary)** | The brand | YES |
| **Subscription-first DTC** (Recharge, Skio, Loop) | The brand | YES (subset) |
| **Marketplace sellers** (Amazon, Shopee, eBay, TikTok Shop) | The marketplace | NO |
| **Wholesale / B2B-primary** | The retailer (Boots, Cult Beauty, Selfridges) | NO |
| **Dropshippers / ad-arbitrage** | Nobody, really | NO |
| **Hybrid retail + online** | Split, retail dominates | NO |

Own-website DTC is the only model where the brand has the email list, the SMS list, the purchase history, and the marketing data. Without those, our entire retention layer is structurally impossible. Marketplace sellers don't even get the customer's contact info, so no Klaviyo flows, no winback, no replenishment, no concierge. Wholesale brands sell to retailers who own the end customer.

We target the brand that owns its own data. That is the bucket we can plug.

### The discovery script (use as-is, in this order)

These are the only five questions. Open, no leading, no pitching. Take notes verbatim where possible.

1. *"What's eating your week right now?"*
2. *"What have you tried for retention, CS, or ops that didn't work, and why?"*
3. *"What are you paying for right now that you wish you didn't have to?"*
4. *"If you could wave a wand and have one thing in your business run itself, what would it be?"*
5. *"Who else do you know running a brand at your stage that I should talk to?"*

If the conversation flows, follow it. If it stalls, return to the next question. End with question 5 always (it's how the funnel grows).

### How to run these calls (discovery FIRST, then pitch if fit)

**First 15-20 minutes: discovery only.** Run the 5 questions. Listen. Take notes. Don't pitch. Don't anchor pricing. Don't talk about workflows. Just collect.

**Around minute 20: transition to pitch if there's clear fit.** Fit = their pain maps to something we can actually build. Use the bucket framing as the bridge:

> *"Most brands at your stage leak money in seven specific places. Sounds like [their specific pain] is your worst leak. Want me to walk you through what we'd build to plug it?"*

**Pricing to anchor (only after they've described their pain):**
- £1k-£2.5k for the build, depending on workflow complexity
- £350/mo retainer (monitoring, docs, workflow discounts, priority queue, monthly strategy call)

**Things to NOT do:**
- Don't pitch in the first 15 minutes. Discovery first, always.
- Don't argue with answers that don't match our hypothesis. Their answers ARE the data.
- Don't anchor pricing before you understand their pain and budget signals.
- Don't pretend we're a productized agency. We're not yet, and they can tell.
- Don't talk about JP.

**Always end with Q5** ("who else should I talk to?") whether they close, decline, or want to think about it. That's how the funnel grows.

### Lock criteria for the 2026-06-05 meeting

We lock the offer at the next meeting based on these specific signals:

1. **Pattern signal:** Did at least 7 of 10 conversations name the same 2 or 3 pain areas? If yes, those are the workflows in v1.
2. **Anomaly signal:** Did any conversations surface a pain we hadn't predicted? If 3+ mentioned it, it joins the v1 menu.
3. **Budget signal:** What did people say they currently pay for and resent? What did they say they'd happily pay for? Productized pricing lands inside that range.
4. **Language signal:** What words did they use to describe the pain? Those words become our marketing copy.
5. **Niche signal:** Should we narrow the subtype (e.g., subscription-first beauty only, supplements-heavy only)? If patterns are bifurcated, we narrow.
6. **Delivery signal:** What did any paid builds delivered during the sprint actually teach us? Which workflows shipped successfully and produced measurable outcomes? Those are the workflows that go into v1 with highest confidence.

If patterns don't emerge from 10 calls + the builds, we run 5 more conversations before locking. Better to delay the lock than lock the wrong thing.
