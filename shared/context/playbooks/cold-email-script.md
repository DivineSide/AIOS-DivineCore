# Cold Email Script — US test prep / tutoring (settled 2026-06-22)

> Pang's settled cold email sequence for the Instantly campaign. **Permission-first**: email 1 asks to send a video, the Loom only goes out after they reply yes. Two A/B versions of the opener (same first lines, different proof anchor), shared follow-ups, then the Loom-delivery email.
>
> Voice: short, person-to-person ("I"), casual, no em-dashes, no "honest". Variables are Instantly merge fields: `{{firstName}}`, `{{Cleaned Company Name}}`. Pairs with [`cold-call-script.md`](cold-call-script.md) (the call is email-first, so it follows this) and the mechanics in [`outbound-playbook.md`](outbound-playbook.md).
>
> **On record:** the proof lines ("15 new referrals from happy parents" / "25 leads" / "saving a client hours a week") are anchors Pang chose to use. They are not yet backed by a verified test-prep result. Flagged repeatedly; kept by Pang's decision. Swap to the forward-framed version ("the strategy I use to...") if a prospect ever asks "which client?".

## Follow-ups

## Email 2 — the Loom delivery (only after they reply yes)

Record the Loom personalized to their center, then send the link with timestamp callouts (ListKit style).

> **subject:** Re: quick question
>
> Hey {{firstName}}, here's the quick video I put together for {{Cleaned Company Name}}, just click the thumbnail to watch:
>
> [Loom thumbnail]
>
> At the 0:30 mark I show where a nearby center is beating you on Google reviews, and around 1:15 I point out where you're losing new inquiries before they book.
>
> How does tomorrow or Thursday at 2 EST work for a quick call so we can walk through it in more detail? If it's easier, grab a time here: [calendar link]
>
> Pang

### The Loom itself (60-90 sec): show where you can help, not how

Their site and a competitor's listing on screen. Give the diagnosis, withhold the fix.

1. **Intro:** "Hey {{firstName}}, Pang here. I looked at {{Cleaned Company Name}} next to a couple of nearby centers, let me show you what I found."
2. **Gap 1, reviews:** "You've got [#] Google reviews, [competitor] has [#]. When a parent searches, that's a big part of who gets called first. That's one place we can help."
3. **Gap 2, follow-up or referrals:** "I couldn't find a fast way for a new parent to get a response, and no system asking your happy parents for referrals. Both are leaving students on the table. More places we can help."
4. **Where the competitor is ahead:** "[Competitor] is also doing [X] that you're not, which pulls students their way."
5. **Close, no how:** "I'm not going to get into exactly how we'd fix these here, the right fix depends on your business. But they're very fixable and they're costing you students now. If you want, I'll walk you through what we'd do on a quick 15 minutes. Tomorrow or Thursday at 2?"

## The manual motion (separate from this automated sequence)

For best-fit and "unicorn" targets, Brendan's manual method: 10-20 researched companies a day, multi-thread top-down (owner, then marketing, then admissions), personalized per company. Same permission-first + Loom structure, but the email 1 opener references a specific thing from your research instead of just the merge fields. The automated sequence above is the volume play; this is the high-touch play.

**Research each center, then lead with one of two angles.** Both reframe the pitch around protecting the word-of-mouth they already earned, not "marketing" (these owners tune marketing out). Reviews are word-of-mouth, just the version that happens when the owner isn't in the room: a referred parent looks them up before deciding, and thin or unanswered reviews are where that referral leaks out.

**Reviews angle:**

> "You've clearly built this on word-of-mouth. The thing is, when a referred parent looks you up, the first thing they check is your Google reviews, and right now that's where some of those referrals quietly drop off."

**Missed-call angle (strongest when you have proof you called and got no answer):**

> "I actually tried calling [Center] earlier this week and couldn't get through. I'm guessing I'm not the only one, and when a referred parent calls ready to sign up and no one picks up, that's a word-of-mouth lead gone to whoever answered first."
