## 01 · LinkedIn-Specific Writing Rules

(Persona-level writing rules — short sentences, no emojis, numbers everywhere, no corporate language — live in [`pang.md`](../identity/pang.md). The rules below are LinkedIn-platform-specific extensions on top.)

### Hook Principles (from LinkedIn course / Chris Walker)

- The hook is the most important part. Its job: get them to click "show more."
- Show credibility + say something bold enough they have to keep reading.
- Always write as if no one has heard of you before — build maximum credibility fast.
- Use as many numbers as possible in hooks. LinkedIn loves numbers.
- Polarizing enough to stand out, but not so extreme it alienates.

### Closing Principles

- End with a coy close that gives people a dopamine hit.
- Do NOT say "Let's continue the conversation in the comments" — that's 2012.
- Do NOT ask a generic question just to prompt engagement.
- The closer should feel like the punchline or the payoff.

### LinkedIn-specific banned phrases

(Canonical brand-level banned-words list lives in [`voice.md`](../identity/voice.md). LinkedIn-specific additions on top.)

- "Let's connect" / "Let's chat"
- "Drop a 🚀 if you agree"
- "Hot take:" (just *make* the take)
- "Have you ever wondered..." / "What if I told you..."
- "Thoughts?" as a closer

### Differentiation Requirement

**Every LinkedIn post must make at least one explicit distinction between what DivineSide builds and what generic "AI automation agencies" build.** Without it, the post reads like every other AI-automation flex on the feed and the brand gets sorted into the same bucket as them.

**Why:** Posts that show *what was built* without naming *what makes our approach structurally different* don't move the brand forward. The LinkedIn audience already has ten of those in their feed every day. We need to look like a different category, not a better version of the same one. This is the rule that protects against the "this just sounds like every other automation post" failure mode.

**How to apply.** Pick at least one of these per post — not all four, but at least one, surfaced clearly enough that a skimmer catches it:

- **The context layer.** We don't run AI on a one-off prompt. We build a context folder *with* the founder — markdown files covering their customers, business, strategy, voice, competitors, team, SOPs, lead channels. Every workflow reads from this folder before it makes a decision. The AI knows the business; it isn't guessing. (Source: [`offer.md`](../sales-and-delivery/offer.md) §02 Phase 1)
- **Embedded expertise.** We feed each workflow the public frameworks of domain experts — media buyers, copywriters, retention strategists, whoever owns the discipline that workflow lives in — so the AI makes decisions the way an expert would, not the way a generic chatbot would. (Source: [`../../CLAUDE.md`](../../../CLAUDE.md) §5)
- **Operating system, not standalone workflow.** We build a layer that wraps the entire business. Each new workflow compounds on the brain we built for the previous one — workflow #2 starts smarter than workflow #1, workflow #3 starts smarter than #2. Standalone automations don't compound. (Source: [`offer.md`](../sales-and-delivery/offer.md) §07)
- **Skin-in-the-game guarantee.** 0% upfront, 100% on delivery. If we miss the target (functions delivered by week 8, or 10 hrs/week saved by month 2 when applicable), you don't pay anything and I pay you $500 out of pocket. Most "AI agencies" charge upfront with no risk on either side. (Source: [`offer.md`](../sales-and-delivery/offer.md) §04 and [`guarantee.md`](../sales-and-delivery/guarantee.md))

**Naming discipline still applies.** Per [`offer.md`](../sales-and-delivery/offer.md) §01, default to leading with the outcome (*"your business, running on AI"*) rather than the category noun (*"AI Operating System"*). Exception: foundational/announcement posts where the category itself is the subject — those earn the term by being the introduction (e.g. the 2026-05-11 pivot post).

## 06 · The Writing System (Full Flow)

The core problem: most people try to generate ideas and write the post at the same time. That's why it takes 2 hours and still feels wrong. **Separate the two completely.**

### Step 1 — Build an Idea Bank (ongoing)

Keep a running notes list at all times. Never start a writing session from zero. Sources to pull from:

- Questions you get asked — in DMs, on calls, in comments
- Personal stories — moments from the build, failures, wins, decisions
- Trending topics and questions in your niche — Reddit (r/ecommerce, beauty-brand operator threads), LinkedIn comments, beauty operator Slack groups, influencer posts
- Case study wins — client results, magic moments from a DivineSide build, teardowns of what stopped working in a beauty brand's ops

### Step 2 — Pick Topic + Post Type → Framework Auto-Selects

Before writing anything, decide what type of post this is. The framework follows automatically:

| Post Type | Funnel Stage | Use Framework |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| Educational / awareness | Top of funnel | **Problem Agitate Solution (PAS)** |
| Personal story / lesson | Middle of funnel | **Story Lesson Application (SLA)** |
| Proof / conversion | Bottom of funnel | **Case Study Framework** |

### Step 3 — First Draft with AI

Give the AI: the framework name + your raw bullet-point thoughts on the topic. Get a draft. **Never post the raw output.**

### Step 4 — Edit for Your Voice

- Add your signature words and phrases (the ones you actually use when speaking)
- Inject a specific story or example that only you could write
- Kill all AI red flags (see Section 08)
- Check for one consistent through line — cut every sentence that goes off on a tangent

### Step 5 — Generate 5 Hook Alternatives

After the draft is done, ask for 5 pattern-interrupting hook alternatives. Pick the one that makes you feel something. Mix and match the best parts of different hooks.

### Step 6 — Batch on Sundays (2–3 hours)

Write a full week's posts in one session. No context switching. When you're in writing mode, keep going.

- **Friday** = personal story post (SLA framework)
- **Rest of week** = educational posts (PAS framework)
- **One case study per week** when you have the material

## 07 · The 3 Post Frameworks

### Framework 1: Problem Agitate Solution (PAS)

**Use for**: educational posts, awareness content, top-of-funnel.

The agitation is the engine — if they don't feel the pain, they don't care.

| Part | What it does | Example (DivineSide-flavoured) |
|------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Problem** | State the problem plainly. One sentence. | *"Your beauty brand is doing $1.2M but your ops manager is still copy-pasting tracking numbers into Klaviyo at midnight."* |
| **Agitate** | Make them feel the pain. Show the consequence. Must be relatable. | *"Every hour she spends on this is an hour she's not building the post-purchase flow that actually retains customers. You're paying $80k/year for someone to do data entry."* |
| **Solution** | Clear, specific steps. Numbered list works well. End with a CTA. | *"Three things an AI OS can replace in your ops stack this month: 1) order-to-tracking pipeline, 2) review-request triggers, 3) low-stock vendor alerts. DM me 'OS' for the teardown."* |

**Biggest mistake**: the agitation isn't relatable. People skim past it. Make them wince.

### Framework 2: Story Lesson Application (SLA)

**Use for**: trust building, relationship content, middle-of-funnel. Your Friday post format.

The application part is optional — sometimes the lesson lands harder alone. But always have the story and the lesson.

| Part | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **Story** | A real moment from your life — the failure, the win, the realization, the stuck point. Not a parable. A specific thing that happened. |
| **Lesson** | The single takeaway. One idea only. What do you want someone to remember from this post? |
| **Application** | 2–3 concrete actions the reader can take now so they don't make the same mistake. Optional — use only when it adds something. |

### Framework 3: Case Study (Bottom of Funnel)

**Use for**: proof, conversion, getting people to DM or sign up.

Always needs a visual — a screenshot, message, or metric. Proof people can see beats any caption.

| Part | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **Hook** | Show the transformation immediately — before vs after numbers in the first line. The hook must carry the proof. |
| **Before** | What was this brand struggling with before they worked with DivineSide? Be specific — hours, dollars, manual steps, errors. |
| **After** | What results did they get? Numbers, revenue, time saved — concrete. |
| **What changed** | The steps you took together. What specifically caused the transformation? The actual AI OS modules deployed. |
| **CTA** | Clear single ask. Add some urgency — limited spots, deadline, specific number. People are busy. Give them a reason to act now. |
| **Visual** | Screenshot of a DM, message, dashboard, or metric. Non-negotiable. Proof people can see beats any caption. |

## 08 · AI Red Flags to Kill Before Posting

Never post a raw AI draft. Read it out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, rewrite it.

These are the telltale signs:

- Rhetorical questions — "Have you ever wondered..." / "What if I told you..."
- "It's not X, it's Y" sentence structure
- Corporate jargon and overly formal language
- Vague statements that sound smart but don't actually mean anything
- Banned words (canonical list in [`voice.md`](../identity/voice.md)): "game changer," "changed everything," "leverage" (verb), "unlock," "empower," "synergy," "robust," "scalable," "transform your business"

**The fix**: read every post out loud. If a sentence sounds like a press release, cut it or rewrite it in one plain sentence.

## 11 · Quick Pre-Post Checklist

Run through this before every post goes live:

- [ ] Does the hook have a number or a specific claim?
- [ ] Does it pass the 'so what?' test — is there a real insight here?
- [ ] Is every sentence necessary? Cut anything that's filler.
- [ ] Does it hit **both non-negotiable anchors** (*"19-year-old"* and *"UK e-commerce beauty brands"*) plus at least one optional anchor? (Locked 2026-05-20. See Constant Reinforcement in [`pang.md`](../identity/pang.md). DivineSide brand name stays OUT of post copy unless the post is explicitly marketing.)
- [ ] Does it draw at least one explicit distinction between DivineSide and generic "AI automation agencies" — context layer, embedded expertise, operating-system framing, or the guarantee? (See §01 Differentiation Requirement)
- [ ] Is the closing strong — not a generic question?
- [ ] No corporate buzzwords. No emojis (unless intentional). No exclamation marks.
- [ ] Would a smart, busy beauty brand operator stop scrolling for this?

If yes to all 8 — post it.

| **Calls booked** | **2 by end of week 1** | (Composite of everything above) |

These are rough heuristics, not promises. The point: when calls booked is low, the playbook gives you ONE place to look — the step where actuals fall furthest below benchmark.

### 12.7 · Free-work cold DM upgrade (cross-channel, sourced 2026-05-26)

Aean's Dream 100 method, applied to LinkedIn. Companion section: [x-playbook.md §11.5](x-playbook.md) covers the cross-channel framing (multi-platform hit on email + IG + X + LinkedIn). This section is the LinkedIn-specific execution.

**The principle:** for every 100 ICP prospects reached out to with free work upfront, 1-2 close (when done properly). Free work differentiates you from the thousands of low-effort cold DMs hitting the same inbox. Sunken-cost effect makes them feel obligated to at least look at it.

**Volume during sprint: 2-3 free-work cold DMs/day on LinkedIn**, plus 3-5 research-question DMs/day for top-of-funnel.

#### What "free work" means for Pang

| Asset | Use case | Time per piece |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| 1-page bucket audit (personalized to that brand) | Default for ICP prospects | 30-45 min after the first 5 calibration runs |
| Loom walkthrough of the audit + 1 workflow to build first | Top-tier ICP — RM365-tier brands | 30-60 min |
| RM365 case study breakdown mapped to their stack | When their tools overlap with RM365 | 20-30 min |

#### Delivery format (sourced from Aean, 2026-05-26)

- Embed the Loom video INSIDE a Google Doc with the written audit
- Send ONLY the Google Doc link in the DM — single trusted link, lower spam flag, cleaner DM
- Doc structure: Loom at top, written audit underneath

#### LinkedIn DM script (3 lines max — anything longer gets clicked off)

> Hey [name], built a quick audit of where AI could replace ops at [their brand]. Loom walks through it. [Google Doc link]
>
> No pitch — share with your team if it's useful.

The "no pitch" line removes implicit sales pressure and earns the click. The "share with your team" framing makes the audit feel like a gift, not a sales artifact.

#### 1-page bucket audit structure (template)

1. **Brand snapshot** (1 paragraph). Their stack signals: Shopify + Klaviyo + which 3PL + which CS tool. AOV / category guess from public signals (product mix, pricing tier, post-purchase email cadence, review volume).
2. **Top 2-3 likely bucket-holes** at their stage: retention gap, CS load, inventory friction — narrowed to the 1-2 most plausible for that specific brand.
3. **The first workflow you'd build** for them: what it does, what it replaces, rough hours/week saved.
4. **What the 8-week pilot would look like** — only include this section if engaging on a follow-up or if the prospect explicitly asked. Don't lead with sales mechanics in the cold-DM audit.

Audit doc lives in Google Drive as a template. Only the brand-specific paragraphs change between sends. Target: ≤30 min/audit after the first 5 (which take 1-2 hrs while calibrating).

#### Follow-up cadence

Day 0 → +1 → +3 → +5 → +7 → +7 → +14 → +14 → +30 → +30 → +30 → then quarterly forever. Same cadence as [x-playbook.md §11.6](x-playbook.md). Most replies land on follow-up #3-5, not message #1. The money is in the follow-up.

Track in a Trello kanban (Outreach Sent → Followed Up → Replied → Call Booked → Closed → Dead) or in the same Google Sheet as the §12.3 tracking. Don't use HubSpot — over-engineered for early stage.

### 12.8 · Long-term cadence — quarterly review

Locked 2026-05-28 as Pang's standing LinkedIn rhythm. The daily and weekly cadences in §12.1 to §12.7 are not sprint-scoped. They hold through quarterly reviews:

| Date | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| 2026-06-05 | Customer-discovery sprint lock meeting. Calls KPI in §12.0 resets here based on what the 10 discovery conversations revealed. Activity cadences hold. |
| 2026-09-05 | Q3 review. Compare 3-month actuals to targets. Adjust only with evidence. |
| 2026-12-05 | Q4 review. Same. |
| 2027-03-05 | Q1 2027 review. Same. |

**Adjustment rules at each review:**

- Look for sustained signal, not single-week noise. Three consecutive weeks above or below benchmark is signal.
- Don't drop below the published cadence without explicit decision in a review meeting. Discipline erodes when targets get nudged in the moment.
- If a cadence component (e.g. 20 connection requests/day) isn't converting, swap reps to a different component (e.g. more free-work DMs) at the same total time cost. Don't just cut the activity.

The 4-calls-by-2026-06-05 KPI in §12.0 is sprint-bound by date. The post-sprint version (calls per month at standing rhythm) gets set at the 2026-06-05 lock meeting based on what's converting.
