## Why each filter

**Independent, owner-operated**
Franchise locations (Kumon, Mathnasium, Sylvan, Huntington) have corporate-mandated tools and standardized curricula. They cannot freely adopt a new system even if the owner wants to. Exclude them from cold email entirely. They are a separate warm-intro track only (see below).

**Local**
This is the filter that does the work of "online vs offline" without the vagueness. "Local" means they serve a specific geographic community — whether in a classroom or over Zoom. It excludes national online course platforms (Udemy instructors, large digital academies) that have no community-rooted ops complexity. A center running classes over Zoom for students in one metro area is still "local" and still our target.

**Test prep and tutoring companies**
This is the US terminology. "Coaching center" is an India/South Asia term. Americans call these businesses "tutoring center", "learning center", "test prep company", or put "Academy" in their business name. Using the wrong term kills credibility in copy.

**Group classes (not 1:1)**
1:1 private tutors are solo operators with no budget, no operational scale, and no staff. Group-based centers run batches of students, manage scheduling at volume, handle parent communication, and deal with lead follow-up — the ops pain is there. This is the automation opportunity.

**2–20 staff**
Below 2 is a solo operator without budget. Above 20 they likely have a dedicated admin or ops person already, making the sell harder and the pain less acute. The sweet spot is the owner still doing ops themselves alongside 2–5 staff.

**$500k–$3M revenue**
Below $500k the owner probably cannot afford a meaningful retainer. Above $3M they may have a real ops infrastructure in place. This band catches businesses with real volume and real chaos, but no dedicated systems person.

## Who we exclude and why

| Excluded | Why |
|----------|-----|
| 1:1 private tutors | Solo operators, no scale, no ops complexity, no budget |
| Kumon / Mathnasium / Sylvan / Huntington franchise locations | Corporate-mandated tools, no freedom to adopt outside systems |
| National online course platforms (Udemy sellers, large digital academies) | Not locally rooted, already have content infrastructure, different buyer profile |
| Pure Kajabi / Teachable course creators | Different business model, content-first not operations-first, lower ops chaos |

**Franchise owners as a separate track**
Franchise owners (e.g., someone who owns 2 Mathnasium locations) are worth pursuing but not via bulk cold email — volume is too low. The upside is their network: if you do good work for one franchise owner, they refer you to 10 peers in the same system. Treat them as a warm LinkedIn outreach track only, not the cold email list.

## Why we do not filter by online vs offline

This question came up twice and gets resolved the same way each time. The real split is not delivery mode — it is whether the business is locally rooted or nationally distributed:

- Pure offline (physical classroom only) has fewer automation touchpoints than online-heavy centers, but the pain is still there (reviews, referrals, lead follow-up).
- Pure online course creators (Kajabi/Teachable) already have content infrastructure and are not operationally broken in the ways we can fix. We are NOT targeting them.
- The pain we solve — lead follow-up, parent communication, re-enrollment, reviews — exists whether the center delivers in a classroom or over Zoom.
- "Local" draws the right line: it excludes national course platforms without restricting centers that happen to use Zoom.
- "Hybrid" is vague and means nothing in a one-liner or a filter. Drop it.

## What we do NOT know yet (as of 2026-06-07)

**The entry pain / entry workflow is TBD.** We have not validated what the biggest operational pain is for this ICP. Hypotheses:

1. Lead follow-up — parents inquire via Google/Yelp/website, manual follow-up, low conversion
2. Parent communication — progress updates, test reminders, re-enrollment nudges at volume
3. Review and referral generation — word of mouth is their main growth channel but there is no system for it

Do NOT assume Target Academy's pain (content/PPT creation) translates to US centers. US test prep centers use published past papers and prep books — they do not create original content. Content automation is an India-specific entry, not a US one.

The entry pain must be validated through research and discovery calls before locking an offer.
