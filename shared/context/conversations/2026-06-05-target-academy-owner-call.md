## Context

8-year coaching-institute owner, ex-teacher turned business owner, deeply understands the product (teaching) side. Runs online courses for government / executive-officer exams (entire-syllabus batches, plus quizzes/question papers as upsells). A long-standing connection to both Mayank and Shubham ("like a brother / uncle"), close enough to be empathetic and motivated to help. Treated as a **distribution channel, not just a client**: he has 50+ convertible connections in the same sector and is more motivated to share a product that solves his own pain than to be sold to.

## Stack snapshot

- **Delivery:** online lectures. Teasers/one class on YouTube, full course gated behind his own app (monetization).
- **His own app:** custom-built by a hired software engineer. Students enrolled there; all announcements, study material, class PPTs, and live classes live in it. This is his broadcasting + management layer.
- **Distribution (his words, ranked):** word-of-mouth (best), past students (he was a renowned teacher first), Google Maps / SEO, marketing posters, WhatsApp + Telegram marketing (one Telegram channel of past/renowned students he keeps posting to), Meta ads. He does many channels, none dedicated.
- **Staff:** separate paid staff just to answer parent calls and keep the fee register updated.

## The four functions: signal

- **Product (education):** the hot one. Content production is all manual: a separate team writes PDFs, makes PPTs, makes videos, writes quizzes/question papers. Named **unprompted** as his single most time-consuming activity. Wants it all generated into his PowerPoint so he can print and hand to students immediately.
- **Marketing:** he does it everywhere (Insta/YT/Facebook/posters) but it is hard for him (no editing experience). He kept insisting on content/marketing help even though his actual growth channel is word-of-mouth.
- **Customer service:** lots of inbound queries (fees, which batch). A separate staffer handles parent calls + fee chasing.
- **Sales:** not a stated problem; he gets students via word-of-mouth and cannot fully meet existing demand.

## The lessons (most important section)

1. **The validated paid need on the India side is content automation (Product function), not the PDF's proposed entry workflow.** The pre-discovery PDFs recommended leading with predictive dropout intervention (Phase-2 PDF) or the marketing result-announcement engine (niche brief). The live paying client rejected dropout detection outright and named content automation unprompted. **Trust the client over the pre-discovery PDFs on entry-workflow.**
2. **"Fixes revenue via marketing" is weak for this client.** His channel is word-of-mouth, his bottleneck is supply/manual work, not leads. A **referral system** (which he liked) fits his real channel better than social content. The direct-P&L workflow question (Shubham's push) is unresolved and goes into the deep-research phase.
3. **Proof-first beats pitch-first.** The trust gap with AI is real; a demo on his own business is the wedge.
4. **He is a distribution channel.** 50+ convertible connections + high motivation to share. Treat the build as the on-ramp to that network, not a one-off.
