## 01 · TL;DR

The **individual workflow tools are saturated.** Klaviyo, Rebuy, Gorgias, Octane, Recharge, Okendo — every £1-5M UK DTC beauty brand is already running most of them. We don't compete with those tools. We use them.

The **JP-shape** — *managed service, vertical-locked to UK DTC beauty £1-5M, all workflows bundled under one retainer* — is **genuinely empty as of May 2026.** No competitor was found running that exact shape in US or UK.

The real budget competition is **UK retention agencies** (Underground Ecom, Sticky Digital, Melusine). They focus on *retention only*. We do *retention + CS + reviews + replenishment + post-purchase* as one bundle. AI is what makes that breadth economically possible at a price one of those agencies would charge for retention alone.

## 02 · Our positioning, sharpened

> **"Most agencies focus on one part of your stack: just retention, or just CS, or just reviews. We set up and run all of them as one system. AI is what makes it economically possible to run all of them on a single retainer."**

The differentiator is **breadth** (we do all of them) vs **depth** (they do one). AI is the *mechanism* that lets a small team operate that breadth, not the marketing line itself.

On calls, the short version:

> *"Underground will run your retention. Octane will run your concierge. Gorgias will help with CS. We do all of it on one retainer."*

Why this lands: it's an **observable, defensible claim** (you can verify the bundle exists). "We use AI agents instead of humans" sounds smart but is **hard to prove and easy to copy.** The bundle is the visible thing the buyer pays for. The AI is how we deliver it. Don't confuse the two.

## 03 · The four layers of competition

### Layer 1 — Component tools (Klaviyo, Rebuy, Gorgias, Octane AI, Recharge, Okendo)

**What they are.** The software apps any £1-5M Shopify beauty brand is already paying for. Klaviyo for email/SMS, Gorgias for CS, Recharge for subscriptions, Octane for pre-purchase quizzes, Okendo for reviews, Rebuy for post-purchase + replenishment.

**How they apply to us.** We don't compete with these. **We use them.** Our job is to set them up properly, connect them into one operating layer, run them on the brand's behalf, and add AI-driven logic on top so they perform harder than a generic out-of-the-box install would.

**The objection we'll hit on calls.** *"We already have Klaviyo, why pay you?"*

**The answer.** *"You have Klaviyo, but it's running 3 generic flows you set up 18 months ago. We'll have it running 12 personalised flows with AI-written copy, tuned weekly against the data. Same tool, several times the output. And we're doing the same on Gorgias, Okendo, Rebuy, and Recharge at the same time."*

### Layer 2 — UK retention agencies (Underground Ecom, Sticky Digital, Melusine)

**What they are.** Agencies that set up and manage Klaviyo flows for DTC brands. Productized, human-executed, multi-vertical. **Underground Ecom was Klaviyo Agency of the Year 2025.** This is the most credentialed segment of the market.

**How they apply to us.** **This is our real budget competitor.** A beauty founder weighing a £4k/mo Underground retainer against ours will not see the difference unless we draw it sharply.

**How we differentiate.** They focus on **retention only**. We do **retention + CS + reviews + replenishment + post-purchase** as one bundle. A founder going with a Klaviyo agency still needs separate agencies (or in-house hires) for CS automation, reviews, and replenishment. With us they get all of them on one retainer.

The line on a call:

> *"Underground is brilliant at Klaviyo flows. But if you hire them, you still need someone running CS, someone managing reviews, someone setting up replenishment. With us, that's one retainer instead of four."*

**Do NOT lean on "AI agents vs human strategists" as the differentiator.** It sounds smart and is genuinely true for our delivery, but it's not the visible thing the buyer pays for and it's easy to copy in marketing copy alone. The bundle is the visible, defensible claim.

### Layer 3 — Other AI agencies for ecom (Vuzo, Tom&Co, Underwater Pistol, Flex Commerce, Medito)

**What they are.** Other small UK agencies marketing themselves as "AI for ecom." Most are doing one-off projects (£15k-£50k for a custom build). **Vuzo (Surrey)** is the only one that has productized — selling AI products at £349-£600 with £600/mo retainers.

**How they apply to us.**
1. **Vuzo's £349 anchor is a price-war threat.** A founder comparing on price alone will see us as 3-7x more expensive.
2. **The project-mode consultancies (Tom&Co, Underwater Pistol)** target bigger brands (£10M+ Shopify Plus). Not direct competitors at our ICP today but could move down-market.

**How we differentiate.**
- **Vertical lock** — UK DTC beauty only. None of these are vertical-specific. The playbooks compound across our brands; theirs don't.
- **Bundle, not products.** Vuzo sells one product at a time (ReviewLab, Blue Canary, GeoLogic). We sell the full operating layer. Different category of buyer.

The line on a call:

> *"Vuzo sells you one AI product at a time. We run your whole operations stack. Different shape, different price."*

### Layer 4 — Agentic AI platforms (Lindy, Relevance AI, n8n, Crew AI, Make, Zapier Central)

**What they are.** Tools that let *anyone* build AI agents. Like Zapier for AI. Lindy markets 3000+ integrations and no-code agent builders.

**How they apply to us.** **They are not competitors today, but they will commoditize the build layer in 12-18 months.** A future ex-client could hire a freelancer with Lindy and rebuild what we built for them.

**How we hedge.** Our moat is not the technical build itself. The moat is:

1. **Vertical knowledge** — we know what works specifically for UK DTC beauty because we'll have shipped to 10+ of them. A generalist Lindy freelancer cannot match the playbook depth in this niche.
2. **We run it ongoing** — the brand doesn't have to think about it. They pay one fee and the system runs. A freelancer build is a one-off; we are continuous operation.
3. **The bundle replaces a hire, not just one tool.** That's a different unit-economic comparison than "buy a Lindy subscription and DIY."

The line if a prospect asks "couldn't I just build this with Lindy?":

> *"You could. You'd also need to figure out what to build, run it once it's built, fix it when Shopify changes an API, and keep it tuned. That's the job. The build is the easy part."*

**This is why staying vertical-locked matters.** Generalists will beat us on price for build-only work. We win on vertical operating discipline. Drop the vertical lock and we lose this argument.

## 04 · The Shopify platform risk (separate from the four layers)

Shopify is building this surface into Shopify itself: **Sidekick** (AI copilot), **Magic** (AI writing), and **Agentic Storefronts** (Winter 2026 release) will integrate agents directly into the platform.

**Likely casualty within 18 months: pre-purchase concierge** (the "skin quiz" / "ingredient questions" / product-matching workflow). Octane AI's flagship use case is exactly what Shopify will absorb.

**Operational consequence:** **do not anchor the v1 bundle on pre-purchase concierge.** Lean on the four workflows with longer competitive runway:

1. **Replenishment** (cross-brand subscription logic, AI-tuned timing)
2. **Lapsed winback** (segmented, AI-personalised, multi-channel)
3. **Post-purchase nurture** (review collection + cross-sell + education sequencing)
4. **CS automation** (Gorgias-integrated triage + AI drafting + escalation logic)

Pre-purchase concierge can stay in the menu as an *add-on* the brand can opt into, but should not be the headline workflow that justifies the retainer.

## 05 · Destination vs entry — how breadth applies in Phase 1

The **destination** (Phase 3 per [CLAUDE.md §2](../../CLAUDE.md)): every client pays one monthly fee, we run the full operating layer, they don't think about which tools or workflows. Like JP's gym system.

The **entry** (Phase 1, current): **sell ONE workflow at a time at £1-2.5k + £350/mo** per [`offer.md` §00](offer.md). Three reasons we don't sell the full bundle on day one:

1. **No founder buys 5 workflows from a stranger.** They buy one, see if we can ship, then expand. The retainer expansion in [`offer.md` §07](offer.md) adds £500/mo per additional active workflow.
2. **We don't yet know which 2-3 workflows to bundle.** That's what the [discovery sprint](../../../.claude/alignment-2026-05-22.md) exists to find out.
3. **JP didn't sell the bundle on day one.** He added layers when client work revealed the next gap. Took 7 years. We compress, we don't skip.

**Where the breadth positioning still applies in Phase 1:**

- **Voice on cold email and calls** — we sound like the bundle. *"We run the full retention/CS/reviews/replenishment layer for UK DTC beauty brands."*
- **The natural next workflow is part of the pitch from day one** — the audit doc names the recommended starting workflow AND the next 2-3 in priority order, so the brand sees the bundle as the destination from the first proposal.
- **The £350/mo retainer is the bundle's foundation, scaling up as we add workflows.** It's not "the retainer + the workflow"; it's "the operating layer + each workflow priced inside it."

**What we do NOT do in Phase 1:**

- Don't sell the full bundle on the first paid engagement.
- Don't promise all 5 workflows shipped in the first pilot.
- Don't price as if we were running a productized subscription before we have the data to do so.

## 06 · Anti-pitches — common objections and the line

| Objection | The line |
|---|---|
| *"We already use Klaviyo, why pay you?"* | "You have Klaviyo, but it's underrun. We're going to make Klaviyo work several times harder, while also running Gorgias, Okendo, Rebuy, and Recharge as one system." |
| *"Isn't this just a Klaviyo agency?"* | "Underground will run your retention. We do retention plus CS plus reviews plus replenishment. One retainer instead of four agency relationships." |
| *"Vuzo charges £349, why are you £1k+?"* | "Vuzo sells one AI product. We run your whole operations stack. Different shape, different price." |
| *"Couldn't I just build this with Lindy / n8n / Zapier?"* | "You could. You'd also need to figure out what to build, run it, fix it when Shopify changes an API, and keep it tuned to your brand. The build is the easy part." |
| *"Why not just hire an in-house ops person?"* | "An ops hire is £4-5k/mo all-in for one person doing one workflow at a time. Our retainer is cheaper and runs multiple workflows in parallel, 24/7." |
| *"What about Shopify's new AI features (Sidekick / Agentic Storefronts)?"* | "Shopify's AI handles parts of the storefront. We run the workflows behind it — retention, CS, reviews — which Shopify's tooling doesn't touch. They're complementary." |

## 07 · Implications for the 2026-06-05 lock meeting

The competitive map confirms the **shape** of the productized offer is correct. The **composition** still needs the discovery-sprint data to lock. Specific inputs:

1. **Bundle composition.** Likely 2-3 of {replenishment, lapsed winback, post-purchase, CS automation}. **Drop pre-purchase concierge from v1** — Shopify will absorb that surface within 18 months.
2. **Pricing window.**
   - **Floor:** above Vuzo's £349-£600 product anchor (avoid race-to-the-bottom on tool-level pricing).
   - **Ceiling:** below an in-house ops hire (~£4-5k/mo UK all-in).
   - **Likely landing zone:** £1-3k/mo subscription depending on bundle width.
3. **Positioning copy** in [`../identity/strategy.md`](../identity/strategy.md) and [`offer.md`](offer.md) should be rewritten in the **"operating layer above your stack"** + **"breadth vs depth"** frame, not the "AI tools" frame. The phrase *"AI is how we deliver it, not what we sell"* should land in voice across copy.
4. **Vertical lock is non-negotiable** for the operational moat argument against Lindy-class platforms. Don't dilute the niche to grow faster — that surrenders the only durable moat against future commoditisation.
