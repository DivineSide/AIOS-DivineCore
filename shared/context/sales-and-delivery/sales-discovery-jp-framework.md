# Sales Discovery Call (JP Middleton Framework, Adapted to UK Ecom Beauty/Skincare DTC)

> **PURPOSE.** This is the master sales-call script for the Phase 1 customer-discovery sprint. It applies JP Middleton's 6-step sales process (extracted from his $1.5k/mo gym AI agency close video) to UK ecom consumer health & beauty DTC. Use when you're confident the workflows we discuss would benefit the brand and you intend to pitch a custom workflow build (£1k-£2.5k + £350/mo retainer).
>
> **Use this when:** you've done basic pre-call audit, the brand fits the niche (UK ecom beauty/skincare DTC, £1M-£5M ARR, on Shopify, on 3PL), and you intend to leave the call with either a 24h audit doc commitment or a booked follow-up. **Do not use this for:** purely-validation conversations with non-decision-makers (use a lighter DM/research script instead), or non-niche prospects.

## The 6-step structure (JP's framework)

| Step | Section | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction | Rapport, get them talking |
| 2 | Discovery | Diagnose problem, build doubt, clarify goals, future-pace |
| 3 | Transition | Bridge from their pain to our solution |
| 4 | Pitch | Specific workflow, specific mechanic |
| 5 | Temperature check / committing phase | Confirm they're sold on the solution before pricing |
| 6 | Investment + close | Price, guarantee, commit to 24h audit doc |

---

## 1. INTRODUCTION (0-3 min)

Light rapport. Where they're based, weather, anything human.

Then pivot:

> *"Cool, we can jump into it. To start off, what prompted you to book the call? What's going on?"*

This is the cleanest entry. They'll tell you exactly why they're here. Listen to the language they use, that's the language you mirror later.

---

## 2. DISCOVERY (3-30 min)

Four sub-phases in order. Don't skip ahead.

### A. Diagnose the problem (3-8 min)

After they answer the "why book the call" question, follow with:

> *"Got it. What would you say is the biggest problem right now in growing the business?"*

Listen. Take notes verbatim. Whatever they say, mirror it back tighter than they said it. Example:

> *"So what this sounds like is your repeat customers are drying up and you don't actually know why. Is that right?"*

Then surface decision authority:

> *"Quick one before we go deeper. Do you have a business partner or co-founder? If so, who makes the call on something like this?"*

If decision-maker is not on call, build heavy doubt through the rest of the call, then book a follow-up with the decision-maker. Don't pitch live.

### B. Reverse-engineer the funnel (8-18 min)

> *"Alright, just so I understand the situation, let's reverse engineer what you're doing right now to get customers. Walk me through everything you're doing. Start from the top."*

While they answer, you're listening for the funnel: ads to site to cart to checkout to CS to fulfillment to repeat purchase. Then drill into numbers:

- Last 30 days, how many orders?
- What's your AOV?
- What's your repeat purchase rate (60-day or 90-day)?
- How much you spending on marketing in the last 30?
- Are you actually making money on the marketing, what's your MER?
- What's your CAC look like vs your LTV?

If they don't know the numbers, push them:

> *"Mind if we pull up your Shopify analytics and Klaviyo together? That'll give me a clearer picture and you'll get more out of this call too."*

**Why this works (per JP):** if they don't know their own numbers, they're embarrassed pulling up the dashboard. That embarrassment builds doubt without you having to manufacture it. They start thinking "I should know this." You're now the consultant.

### C. Pillar questions (18-26 min)

These are the workflows we can build. Ask each one. Build doubt where there's a gap.

Bridge questions, don't ask them in isolation.

**1. Reviews**

> *"How are you currently collecting product reviews? Walk me through the process. Is it automated, or is someone manually asking?"*

Then drill: *"What platform, Trustpilot, Okendo, Yotpo, Judge.me? Are you asking timed to delivery or just blanket?"*

Build doubt: *"For beauty, reviews are the single biggest pre-purchase trust signal. If you're sitting under 4.5 with under a couple hundred reviews, that's directly costing you conversion. There's also software that tracks how reviews influence your SEO ranking and how you show up in LLM search like ChatGPT and Perplexity. People search for skincare in LLMs now, the ones with rich review data get cited. The ones without don't exist."*

**2. Referrals (bridge straight from reviews)**

> *"After someone leaves you a great review, do you have a process to ask them for a referral?"*

Almost always no. Then:

> *"Right, so a happy customer who just publicly endorsed you, that's the warmest possible audience for a referral ask, and most brands do nothing with that signal. There's massive opportunity sitting there. Wouldn't you agree?"*

Get them to say yes. Now you've sold the workflow before you've pitched it.

**3. Remarketing old customers (the database goldmine)**

> *"Pull up Klaviyo for me. How many total email subscribers do you have? And how many of them are lapsed, haven't purchased in 90 days?"*

When they tell you, react:

> *"So you have [X] thousand people who already bought from you, already trust you, already gave you money, and right now nothing is happening with them. That's the cheapest revenue you'll ever access and it's just sitting there. There's a massive opportunity to remarket to that list properly. Don't you think there's room for improvement?"*

**4. Replenishment (consumables specific)**

> *"For your bestseller, how long does a customer typically take to finish it? 30, 60, 90 days? Are you sending a replenishment reminder timed to that cycle?"*

If no: *"That's the single highest-ROI flow in beauty. A reminder 7 days before they run out, with a one-click reorder. Brands at your size leave £40-80k a year on the table by not running this."*

**5. Ads and creative (volume + fatigue)**

> *"How are you currently creating ads? Who's making the creative, how often, what's the testing cadence? And how are you determining ad spend, is it gut or based on MER?"*

Listen for: creative fatigue, no UGC pipeline, no systematic testing, founder doing it all themselves.

**6. Lead and inquiry follow-up speed**

> *"When someone DMs you on Instagram with a product question, or fills out a contact form, how long does it take to get a reply? Especially outside of working hours."*

Whatever they say, hit them with:

> *"Harvard Business School ran a study in 2018 showing if you don't follow up within 5 minutes, your chances of converting that lead drop by over 400%. For DTC, every DM left for 6 hours is a lost sale. What's your current process?"*

**7. CS load and WISMO**

> *"Who's handling customer service right now? How many tickets a week? How much of it is WISMO, like 'where's my order'?"*

If WISMO is more than 40% of tickets (it usually is), that's an obvious automation play.

**8. Current AI usage**

> *"How are you currently leveraging AI in the business? What have you tried, what's worked, what hasn't?"*

This question tells you their sophistication level and what they've already burned money on. Crucial for pricing the build later.

### D. Goals + future-pace (26-30 min)

> *"Alright, that's helpful. Where's the business at right now in revenue, and where do you want it to be by end of year or in the next 12 months?"*

Then:

> *"And what happens if nothing changes? If you keep doing what you're doing right now, are you going to hit that?"*

This is the JP "future pace" move. They have to say no out loud. Once they do, they've admitted the gap.

Close discovery with:

> *"Is there anything else I should know to fully understand your situation? I want to make sure I'm not missing anything."*

Their last answer often contains the real objection or the real pain.

---

## 3. TRANSITION (30-32 min)

> *"Alright, that makes sense. So what this sounds like is you need direction from someone who can tell you exactly what to focus on and what to stop wasting time on. Based on what you're doing right now, there's a ton of opportunity sitting in your email database, a ton of opportunity in getting reviews so customers and LLMs find you, and a big opportunity to get referrals because that process isn't there.*
>
> *Given what you're doing, we can definitely help you out. What we do is help UK ecom beauty and skincare brands like yours that don't have control of their growth systems get control over them, so you can grow profitably and consistently. And we use AI to do it. Make sense?*
>
> *I just want to start by saying, what I'm about to walk through is based on what you've been doing, where you are right now, and where you're trying to get. Cool?"*

Mirror their exact words from discovery here. If they said *"we're drowning in DMs,"* use that. If they said *"the email list is dead,"* use that.

---

## 4. THE PITCH (32-38 min)

Lead with the workflow that landed hardest in discovery. Don't try to pitch all 8. Pick 1-2 they most clearly need.

Generic frame, fill in the specific:

> *"Based on what you told me, the highest-leverage place to start is [specific workflow, e.g. the lapsed customer reactivation system]. Here's how it works.*
>
> *I learn your business first. Products, voice, customer language, the tone you use in emails, the way your best-performing flows are written. That becomes the AI brain for your brand. It's specific to you, not generic.*
>
> *Then I connect it to your stack. Shopify for purchase data, Klaviyo for the email list, your reviews platform, whatever else is relevant. Now the brain sees what's actually happening in real time.*
>
> *Then I build the workflow. For [their pain], that means [specific mechanic: e.g. an automated segment of customers who haven't purchased in 90 days, a personalized 4-email sequence in your voice referencing their last purchase, with the right discount logic baked in, fired automatically and monitored weekly].*
>
> *Live, monitored, your team uses it daily. Typical timeline is 2 to 6 weeks depending on scope."*

---

## 5. TEMPERATURE CHECK / COMMITTING PHASE (38-40 min)

Before pricing, you check they're sold on the solution.

> *"Does that match what you were imagining when you booked this call?"*
>
> *"How does that sit with you?"*
>
> *"Anything I'm missing on this?"*

If they're nodding and engaged, move to investment. If they're hesitant, dig:

> *"What's the hesitation? Tell me where I'm not landing."*

Don't push price until they're committed to the solution.

---

## 6. INVESTMENT + CLOSE (40-45 min)

> *"On investment, builds like this run £1k to £2.5k depending on the complexity of the workflow. For what we just talked through, I'd anchor around £[specific], agreed at kickoff before any work starts.*
>
> *After it ships, you go on a £350 a month retainer. That covers monitoring, bug fixes within one business day, a monthly strategy call to figure out the next workflow to build, and a 25% discount on every additional workflow you commission. Month-to-month, no lock-in, opt-out anytime.*
>
> *Payment is 0% upfront, you only pay on delivery. If I don't deliver what we agreed at kickoff, you don't pay anything and I cut you a $500 cheque from my pocket for the time you committed. Real skin in the game."*

Pause. Let them respond.

**Don't fully close live. Commit to a one-page audit doc with the specific scope and pricing within 24 hours, and book the follow-up call before you hang up.**

> *"Here's what I'd like to do. Let me write up exactly what we'd build, the timeline, the price, all the specifics, into a one-page doc. I'll send it tonight. Take a day with it, then let's reconnect [specific day, 2-3 days out] to lock it in. Sound good?"*

Always end with:

> *"Anything else you want to talk through or ask before we wrap?"*

That last question is where the real objection comes out.

## Quick reference: the 8 pillar questions

1. How do you get reviews?
2. How do you get referrals (off the back of reviews)?
3. How do you remarket to lapsed customers?
4. Are you running replenishment for consumables?
5. How are you creating ads and determining ad spend?
6. How fast are you following up on DMs and inquiries (Harvard 5-min)?
7. What's your CS load and WISMO percentage?
8. How are you currently leveraging AI?

## Hard rules

- Don't pitch before minute 30. Discovery first, always.
- Make them pull up Shopify and Klaviyo live. Their embarrassment builds doubt for free.
- Bridge reviews to referrals. Don't ask them separately.
- Always future-pace ("what happens if nothing changes?").
- Mirror their words back when transitioning. Don't use your language.
- No em-dashes, no "co-founder/agency" framing if it's an Upwork-style buyer.
- Don't close live. 24h audit doc + booked follow-up.
- Always end on "anything else?"
